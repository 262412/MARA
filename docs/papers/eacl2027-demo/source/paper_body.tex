\begin{abstract}
LMDoc is a local-first document-QA workbench that records which evidence was retrieved, which route was selected, and why an answer was returned or withheld. Built on Kotaemon, it helps researchers and technical users inspect answer stages, change sources or routes, and test suspected failure causes through Web, command-line, and diagnostic workflows. Two forms of evidence illustrate this use. A historical benchmark bundle spanning six datasets exposes routing failures and trade-offs, with no consistent F1 advantage over fixed text retrieval. Two case studies connect recorded decisions to interventions: replaying the same recorded claims tests a targeted verifier repair, and a public-chart comparison examines the effect of including image evidence. We release the Apache-2.0 implementation, captured case inputs and outputs, reproduction scripts, and a demonstration of the document-QA interface.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Researchers, students, and technical professionals increasingly use retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to ask questions about their own documents \citep{lewis2020rag}. Their collections include prose, tables, figures, and slides. The relevant evidence can therefore depend on both the question and the available document representations. A useful workbench must also let a user inspect what was retrieved, which execution path was chosen, and why an answer was accepted or withheld.

LMDoc (released as MARA) addresses this inspection problem. It extends the open-source Kotaemon application \citep{kotaemon} with a rule-based controller, structured evidence and decision records, and a benchmark harness for examining the same runtime. Users can connect a displayed answer to retrieval, generation, verification, and guardrail decisions, then inspect the effects of changing sources or execution routes.

The historical benchmark illustrates why this inspection matters. The controller's token F1 is slightly below fixed text RAG on QASPER, ALCE-ASQA, and MMDocRAG. It chooses text evidence on all ALCE-ASQA examples and 97\% of QASPER examples, while frequently selecting a weak graph route on RAGTruth. Route frequencies, uncertainty estimates, and latency expose where the policy stays inactive and where its choices warrant investigation.

Our contributions are: (1) a local-first document-QA workbench with Web and CLI access to shared state and runtime records; (2) configurable evidence routing with retrieval and verification diagnostics; and (3) an analysis of a six-dataset benchmark bundle and two case studies that use captured records to test a verifier repair and inspect the effect of visual evidence.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
Self-RAG makes retrieval and critique explicit model decisions \citep{asai2023selfrag}; Adaptive-RAG motivates adaptive retrieval \citep{jeong2024adaptiverag}; and CRAG studies corrective retrieval \citep{yan2024crag}. LMDoc implements these ideas as application rules and diagnostic records. Page-image retrieval \citep{faysse2024colpali} and graph-based retrieval \citep{edge2024graphrag} motivate its evidence paths. Kotaemon supplies the underlying document application. LMDoc's extension connects route decisions, user-visible status, and exported diagnostics. Our quantitative comparisons are between LMDoc configurations; Section~\ref{sec:case} and Appendix~\ref{app:case} examine the added inspection functions alongside inherited capabilities.

The released implementation retains the public commands \texttt{MARA} and \texttt{MARA-cli}, which expose application startup and document-QA operations. The \texttt{MARA docqa} group supports indexing, file inspection, questions, saved conversations, source selection, notes, and derived study artifacts. Optional agent-platform wrappers invoke these commands. The Web and CLI surfaces reuse DocQA settings, indices, and persisted state; the benchmark accesses that runtime through dataset and route adapters.

Kotaemon supplies upload, indexing, model configuration, chat, and evidence presentation. LMDoc adds structured route, retrieval, verification, and guardrail decisions and their connection to CLI and benchmark records. Together, these fields let an analyst locate the stage that changed an answer and examine the corresponding implementation rule.

\paragraph{Local-first deployment.}
Documents, indices, and conversations can be managed locally, with configurable generation and embedding providers. Local-first does not mean that every configuration is offline: using a hosted API sends the corresponding requests and evidence to that provider. A fully local deployment requires local backends for the selected operations. The choice is explicit in configuration, and the benchmark and video use different backend deployments (Section~\ref{sec:demo}).

\subsection{Controller, Evidence, and Verification}
\label{sec:controller}
The controller examines task type, scope, modality cues, available evidence, and backend health. A heuristic planner proposes a route. When modality probes are available, the scorer evaluates the permitted text, page-image, element, hybrid, and global-graph routes. A probe summarizes evidence count, top retrieval score and margin, source/page locator coverage, and text or OCR availability. Direct answers and abstention are separate execution outcomes; they are not additional candidates in the benchmark's automatic scorer.

For a permitted, non-skipped route $r$, the selection score is
\begin{equation}
 S(r)=\max\{0,\widehat Q(r)-\widehat C(r)\}.
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat Q$ and $\widehat C$ are uncalibrated rule-based proxies in internal scoring units. Text, page-image, element, graph, and hybrid routes have base cost penalties of $0.05$, $0.45$, $0.15$, $0.10$, and $0.35$, respectively. Intent and backend availability modify these penalties and the quality proxies. This dataset-aware policy includes rules specific to MMDocRAG and finance. Appendix~\ref{app:rules} gives the formulas, thresholds, skip conditions, and source anchors.



Retrieval adequacy is a heuristic assessment of captured evidence, with additional domain rules. Verification evaluates evidence and citations using lexical, semantic, and domain-specific checks. Light and strict modes apply different unsupported-claim thresholds. The guarded benchmark configuration combines strict verification with thorough retrieval, so its comparison changes more than one setting.

Recovery operates at several stages. The pipeline's additional retrieval attempt requires thorough mode and an empty initial retrieval. The structured controller can also repeat an ambiguous retrieval and examine alternative permitted routes after failed retrieval. Its switch flag records a successful alternative route, potentially reached through several retrieval calls. After generation, verification can invoke answer revision or guardrails. Appendix~\ref{app:rules} details these distinct mechanisms.

Retrieval choice, configured generator identity, and actual generation behavior are separate parts of execution. In the historical benchmark configuration, the automatic controller has a text generator; the fixed page-image comparison configures a visual generator. The implementation also contains OCR-first and evidence-only paths, so a page-image route alone does not identify which generator produced the answer.

\section{Benchmark Diagnostics}
\label{sec:evaluation}
\subsection{Released Evidence and Measurement Scope}
This section reanalyzes the historical v0.0.40 synthesis bundle; Section~\ref{sec:case} presents separate case studies. The bundle contains 41 completed run/shard artifact sets and 3,540 route-level metric records from evaluating 1,090 dataset--question pairs across configurations. The datasets are QASPER \citep{dasigi2021qasper}, ALCE-ASQA \citep{gao2023alce}, MMDocRAG \citep{dong2025mmdocrag}, FinanceBench \citep{islam2023financebench}, RAGTruth \citep{niu2024ragtruth}, and SlideVQA \citep{tanaka2023slidevqa}. Dataset coverage and all configuration-level results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:results}.

The benchmark configures Qwen3-8B for text generation \citep{yang2025qwen3} and bge-m3 for text retrieval \citep{chen2024bgem3}. Multimodal records identify a ColQwen visual retriever; the fixed page-image configuration identifies a local Qwen3-VL generator. These fields record configured backends; actual per-answer generation paths are missing. The run ledger allocates two A100 GPUs per text shard and two L40S GPUs per multimodal shard as experimental resources.

We report the bundle's normalized token F1 and exact match (EM), citation-locator recall, page hit, heuristic unsupported-claim rate, false abstention, recorded route switches, errors, and instrumented time. These local harness diagnostics measure answer overlap, evidence locations, system decisions, and execution behavior. Citation overlap and heuristic support flags require separate factual inspection. Dataset-native fields are retained in the supplement; no official external evaluator was configured.

Paired F1 differences use the same dataset--example identifiers, with 1,000 bootstrap resamples and seed 20260705. Intervals are descriptive 95\% percentile intervals, without a multiple-comparison adjustment. We reproduced all 14 released intervals and reconciled the 20 aggregate rows with the per-example records. A confidence interval crossing zero does not establish equivalence or non-inferiority.

\subsection{What the Traces Reveal}
\paragraph{Text-heavy results mostly exercise text routing.}
Table~\ref{tab:paired} gives the fixed-text comparisons. The controller selects text on 200/200 ALCE-ASQA and 194/200 QASPER questions (Table~\ref{tab:routes}); these results primarily characterize text execution. Auto exceeds the stricter guarded configuration by 1.38 and 10.57 F1 points, with intervals [0.28, 2.68] and [7.30, 13.98]. Guarded false-abstention rates are 6\% and 46\%, versus 0\% for auto, exposing the trade-off introduced by stricter verification and thorough retrieval.

\paragraph{A diagnosable graph-routing failure.}
On RAGTruth, 214/300 controller records select global-graph evidence, with mean F1 19.60; the 83 text-routed records average 27.82, and three executions fail. The paired comparison against text RAG is also negative, with an interval excluding zero. Together, these observations identify frequent selection of the prototype graph route as a failure to investigate. The route-specific subsets contain different questions, so their mean difference alone does not isolate a causal route effect.

\paragraph{Multimodal choice has measurable costs.}
On MMDocRAG, auto's instrumented mean/p95 time is 22.37/101.54 seconds, versus 14.33/38.48 for text RAG. The four controller shards use a separate extended-timeout manifest; comparisons use the common manifest. This limits a controlled latency or reliability comparison. Across all 1,090 controller records, only ten successful switches are recorded (0.92\%): six on MMDocRAG and four on FinanceBench. Recovery is therefore a small part of observed execution.

The per-question best fixed comparison on MMDocRAG averages 43.26 F1, versus 34.60 for auto. The difference of means is 8.66 points; the bundle's mean positive-part regret is 11.05 points because it clips each negative difference to zero. This is a comparison-configuration upper envelope, not a directly attainable routing oracle: the page-image comparison has a different configured generator. On FinanceBench the envelope is 29.35 versus 15.10, but it selects direct-answer or guarded configurations on 101/150 questions, outside auto's selectable route set. Treating that full difference as routing error would be misleading.

\paragraph{SlideVQA exposes probe-driven routing.}
The heuristic planner proposes text on 87/120 questions, but the probe scorer chooses page-image evidence on all 120; recorded visual confidence is 0.77--0.89 and text confidence is zero. These records show probes changing route decisions. Auto and the fixed page-image configuration nevertheless tie on F1, EM, native score, both citation-recall measures, and page hit for every paired example. The comparison is non-discriminating despite different configured generators and generation times. Missing generated answers and executed generation paths leave the cause unresolved, including whether the implementation's OCR-first path contributed to the ties. The equal scores therefore provide no evidence of equivalent visual QA quality.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
LMDoc connects document-QA decisions to inspectable evidence and answer stages. In the case studies, these records locate a lexical verification error, support testing its repair on the same recorded claims, and enable a controlled inspection of image input. The historical benchmark complements these cases by exposing inactive routing, a weak graph route, and execution costs. Together, they demonstrate a workbench for investigating concrete failures.

The scope of this evidence is limited. The benchmark shows no consistent F1 advantage over fixed text RAG, and dataset-aware heuristics and prototype routes constrain transfer. The case interventions concern one verifier error and one manually selected chart; broader accuracy, cost, and recovery gains require further evaluation. A matched Kotaemon experiment or participant study is needed to assess comparative usefulness and user efficiency. Historical generation paths remain unavailable, and minimum local hardware requirements have not been measured.

\section*{Ethics and Broader Impact}
Local storage can help users control documents, but privacy depends on the configured services. Hosted generation or embedding APIs can receive document content; users should choose providers and material appropriate to their permissions and data requirements. Citation and verification indicators can be wrong and should support inspection rather than substitute for expert judgment. We use the existing benchmark artifacts, fictional case documents, and an attributed public-agency chart, without new human-subject data. Public datasets remain subject to their original terms; releasing software or diagnostic records does not grant permission to redistribute every source document. The financial subset evaluates document-QA behavior and is not financial advice.
