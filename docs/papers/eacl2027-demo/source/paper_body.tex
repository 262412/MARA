\begin{abstract}
Document question answering involves choices about evidence, retrieval, generation, and verification that are difficult to inspect together. We present MARA, a local-first workbench extending Kotaemon with observable route control and shared diagnostic records across Web, command-line, and benchmark workflows. We analyze a released bundle covering six datasets, 1,090 dataset--question pairs, and 3,540 route-level records. Automatic routing does not consistently improve answer F1 over fixed text retrieval; the records expose inactive routing, a weak graph route, multimodal latency, and a non-discriminating visual comparison. A separate, executed diagnostic case follows an unresolved question through successful retrieval, a verification-triggered refusal, source correction, and a supported answer. We provide the case's raw provider outputs, displayed and scoring answers, evidence, effective settings, and offline checks. MARA's contribution is an inspectable workflow for locating such failures, with explicit limits on what the retained evidence can reproduce. We release the Apache-2.0 implementation and diagnostic materials, alongside a user-workflow demonstration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Researchers, students, and technical professionals increasingly use retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to ask questions about their own documents \citep{lewis2020rag}. Their collections include prose, tables, figures, and slides. The relevant evidence can therefore depend on both the question and the available document representations. A useful workbench must also let a user inspect what was retrieved, which execution path was chosen, and why an answer was accepted or withheld.

MARA addresses this inspection problem. It extends the open-source Kotaemon application \citep{kotaemon} with an application-level controller, structured evidence and decision records, and a benchmark harness for examining the same runtime. The contribution is an integrated workbench for observing and diagnosing document QA. We do not introduce a trained routing model or claim that automatic routing improves QA accuracy.

This distinction matters empirically. In our released runs, the controller's token F1 is slightly below fixed text RAG on QASPER, ALCE-ASQA, and MMDocRAG. It chooses text evidence on all ALCE-ASQA examples and 97\% of QASPER examples. On RAGTruth it frequently selects a weak graph route. Reporting these outcomes alongside uncertainty, route frequencies, and latency makes the limits of the implemented policy inspectable, rather than treating the existence of routing as evidence of its benefit.

Our contributions are: (1) a local-first document-QA workbench with Web and CLI access to shared state and runtime records; (2) explicit, configurable evidence routing with retrieval and verification diagnostics; and (3) an artifact-backed analysis of a released six-dataset bundle, complemented by an executed diagnostic walkthrough with raw outputs and a reproducible source-selection intervention.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
Self-RAG makes retrieval and critique explicit model decisions \citep{asai2023selfrag}; Adaptive-RAG motivates adaptive retrieval \citep{jeong2024adaptiverag}; and CRAG studies corrective retrieval \citep{yan2024crag}. MARA draws on these decision structures but uses ordinary application rules and records, without reflection-token training. Page-image retrieval \citep{faysse2024colpali} and graph-based retrieval \citep{edge2024graphrag} motivate available evidence paths, rather than constituting new algorithms here. Kotaemon supplies the underlying document application. MARA's specific extension is the connection between route decisions, user-visible status, and exported diagnostics. Our quantitative comparisons are between MARA configurations; the task-level upstream comparison in Table~\ref{tab:boundary} is a static source walkthrough.

The Web interface lets users upload and select documents, inspect a page, ask a question, and follow citations back to evidence (Figure~\ref{fig:ui}). A compact status card shows the selected route, retrieval status, and verification outcome. \paragraph{Local-first deployment.}
Documents, indices, and conversations can be managed locally, with configurable generation and embedding providers. Local-first does not mean that every configuration is offline: using a hosted API sends the corresponding requests and evidence to that provider. A fully local deployment requires local backends for the selected operations. The choice is explicit in configuration, and the benchmark and video use different backend deployments (Section~\ref{sec:demo}).

\subsection{Controller, Evidence, and Verification}
\label{sec:controller}
The controller examines task type, scope, modality cues, available evidence, and backend health. A heuristic planner proposes a route. When modality probes are available, the scorer evaluates the permitted text, page-image, element, hybrid, and global-graph routes. A probe summarizes evidence count, top retrieval score and margin, source/page locator coverage, and text or OCR availability. Direct answers and abstention are separate execution outcomes; they are not additional candidates in the benchmark's automatic scorer.

For a permitted, non-skipped route $r$, the selection score is
\begin{equation}
 S(r)=\max\{0,\widehat Q(r)-\widehat C(r)\}.
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat Q$ and $\widehat C$ are uncalibrated rule-based proxies, not probabilities, measured seconds, or monetary costs. Text, page-image, element, graph, and hybrid routes have base cost penalties of $0.05$, $0.45$, $0.15$, $0.10$, and $0.35$, respectively. Intent and backend availability modify these penalties and the quality proxies. Some rules depend explicitly on the dataset family, including MMDocRAG and finance. Appendix~\ref{app:rules} gives the formulas, thresholds, skip conditions, and source anchors; we do not present this policy as dataset-independent generalization.



Retrieval adequacy is a heuristic assessment of captured evidence, with additional domain rules. Verification uses evidence, citations, lexical/semantic rules, and domain-specific checks; it is not an independent factuality oracle. Light and strict modes apply different unsupported-claim thresholds. The guarded benchmark configuration combines strict verification with thorough retrieval, so its comparison changes more than one setting.

Recovery has distinct levels. The pipeline's additional retrieval attempt requires thorough mode and an empty initial retrieval. Separately, the structured controller can repeat an ambiguous retrieval and examine alternative permitted routes after failed retrieval. It records a successful recovery route when one is found. Thus a single recorded route switch is not a guarantee of a single retrieval call. Verification after generation can invoke revision or guardrails; it should not be conflated with retrieval-triggered route switching. Appendix~\ref{app:rules} details these conditions.

Crucially, selecting page-image evidence does not establish that a VLM generated the answer. In the released configuration the automatic controller has a text generator; the fixed page-image comparison configures a visual generator. The implementation also contains OCR-first and evidence-only paths. We therefore distinguish retrieval choice, configured generator identity, and actual generation behavior.

\section{Benchmark Diagnostics}
\label{sec:evaluation}
\subsection{Released Evidence and Measurement Scope}
This section reanalyzes the existing v0.0.40 synthesis bundle. The new case in Section~\ref{sec:case} is reported separately and does not alter these benchmark results. The historical bundle contains 41 completed run/shard artifact sets and 3,540 route-level metric records for 1,090 dataset--question pairs. These are repeated evaluations across configurations, not 3,540 distinct questions. The datasets are QASPER \citep{dasigi2021qasper}, ALCE-ASQA \citep{gao2023alce}, MMDocRAG \citep{dong2025mmdocrag}, FinanceBench \citep{islam2023financebench}, RAGTruth \citep{niu2024ragtruth}, and SlideVQA \citep{tanaka2023slidevqa}. Dataset coverage and all configuration-level results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:results}.

The benchmark configures Qwen3-8B for text generation \citep{yang2025qwen3} and bge-m3 for text retrieval \citep{chen2024bgem3}. Multimodal records identify a ColQwen visual retriever; the fixed page-image configuration identifies a local Qwen3-VL generator. These are configured backend identities and do not certify which generation path produced each answer. The run ledger allocates two A100 GPUs per text shard and two L40S GPUs per multimodal shard. These allocations are experimental resources, not minimum deployment requirements.

We report the bundle's normalized token F1 and exact match (EM), citation-locator recall, page hit, heuristic unsupported-claim rate, false abstention, recorded route switches, errors, and instrumented time. These are local harness diagnostics; no official external evaluator was configured. In particular, citation-locator overlap is not a measure of entailment, and a zero heuristic unsupported-claim rate is not proof of factual correctness. Dataset-specific native score fields are retained in the supplement but are not treated as official leaderboard scores.

Paired F1 differences use the same dataset--example identifiers, with 1,000 bootstrap resamples and seed 20260705. Intervals are descriptive 95\% percentile intervals, without a multiple-comparison adjustment. We reproduced all 14 released intervals and reconciled the 20 aggregate rows with the per-example records. A confidence interval crossing zero does not establish equivalence or non-inferiority.

\subsection{What the Traces Reveal}
\paragraph{Text-heavy results mostly exercise text routing.}
Table~\ref{tab:paired} gives the fixed-text comparisons. The controller selects text on 200/200 ALCE-ASQA and 194/200 QASPER questions (Table~\ref{tab:routes}). Their high tie counts therefore provide little evidence about the value of selecting among modalities. Auto exceeds the stricter guarded configuration on these datasets by 1.38 and 10.57 F1 points, with intervals [0.28, 2.68] and [7.30, 13.98]. Guarded false-abstention rates are 6\% and 46\%, versus 0\% for auto. This exposes a configuration trade-off rather than establishing a superior verifier.

\paragraph{A diagnosable graph-routing failure.}
On RAGTruth, 214/300 controller records select global-graph evidence, with mean F1 19.60; the 83 text-routed records average 27.82, and three executions fail. These subsets contain different questions, so their difference is not a controlled route-effect estimate. The paired comparison against text RAG is nevertheless negative, with an interval excluding zero. Together, these records identify graph selection as a concrete failure to investigate in a policy that permits a prototype graph route. They do not justify promoting that route as a quality improvement.

\paragraph{Multimodal choice has measurable costs.}
On MMDocRAG, auto's instrumented mean/p95 time is 22.37/101.54 seconds, versus 14.33/38.48 for text RAG. The four controller shards use a separate extended-timeout manifest; comparisons use the common manifest. This limits a controlled latency or reliability comparison. Across all 1,090 controller records, only ten successful switches are recorded (0.92\%): six on MMDocRAG and four on FinanceBench. Recovery is therefore a small part of observed execution.

The per-question best fixed comparison on MMDocRAG averages 43.26 F1, versus 34.60 for auto. The difference of means is 8.66 points; the bundle's mean positive-part regret is 11.05 points because it clips each negative difference to zero. This is a comparison-configuration upper envelope, not a directly attainable routing oracle: the page-image comparison has a different configured generator. On FinanceBench the envelope is 29.35 versus 15.10, but it selects direct-answer or guarded configurations on 101/150 questions, outside auto's selectable route set. Treating that full difference as routing error would be misleading.

\paragraph{SlideVQA diagnoses routing, not generator quality.}
The heuristic planner proposes text on 87/120 questions, but the probe scorer chooses page-image evidence on all 120; recorded visual confidence is 0.77--0.89 and text confidence is zero. This is evidence that probes can change a route decision. However, auto and the fixed page-image configuration have identical F1, EM, native score, both citation-recall measures, and page hit on every paired example. Different configured generators and generation times do not make this comparison discriminating. The implementation has an OCR-first path, and the metric-only bundle omits generated answers and actual generation-path traces. We cannot identify the cause from these records and do not use the equality as evidence of equivalent visual QA quality.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
MARA provides an inspectable workbench and an artifact-backed way to diagnose route behavior. The current evidence does not establish higher QA accuracy, lower cost, a reliable factuality guarantee, or a performance advantage over unmodified Kotaemon. The author-constructed case establishes one inspectable diagnostic sequence, not a user-study benefit or general recovery rate. Dataset-aware heuristics, missing historical execution provenance, prototype routes, and the non-discriminating visual comparison constrain generalization. Minimum local hardware requirements have not been measured. Preserving these negative findings is central to the diagnostic contribution: users and developers can see which decisions warrant improvement and which conclusions the current records cannot support.

\section*{Ethics and Broader Impact}
Local storage can help users control documents, but privacy depends on the configured services. Hosted generation or embedding APIs can receive document content; users should choose providers and material appropriate to their permissions and data requirements. Citation and verification indicators can be wrong and should support inspection rather than substitute for expert judgment. We use the existing benchmark artifacts and author-constructed fictional case documents, without new human-subject data. Public datasets remain subject to their original terms; releasing software or diagnostic records does not grant permission to redistribute every source document. The financial subset evaluates document-QA behavior and is not financial advice.
